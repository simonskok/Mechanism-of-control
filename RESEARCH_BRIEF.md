# Research brief: the evidence layer

Paste this whole file into Claude or ChatGPT. Everything the model needs is inside it. Work
one track at a time rather than all six at once, and return results in the shapes given at the
end of each track.

---

## What you are being asked to do

There is a website about how power sustains itself without appearing as power. It explains the
arguments well. It has almost no evidence of those arguments operating in the world.

Every mechanism on it is currently illustrated by the example the theory arrived with:
Bentham's prison, Havel's greengrocer, Illich's car, the Prussian *Volksschule*. Those are
teaching examples from books written between 1548 and 2019. They are not evidence that any of
this is happening now, to anyone in particular, through any named institution.

Your job is to supply what is missing: documented cases, real employers, real money, real
distribution channels, and an honest map of where people are most exposed today. Not
quotations. Not summaries of the theory. Cases with dates, names and records.

## The project, in five lines

The site covers six thinkers (Bourdieu, Gramsci, Schmitt, Foucault, Illich, Agamben), five
domains of control (education, media, law, psychology, bureaucracy), twelve institutional
milieus from the Macy Conferences to the Mont Pelerin Society, and a comparison of Eastern and
Western regimes of control. It is written for readers roughly 18 to 40 with no background in
social theory. It is organized by institution rather than by person, because who paid for the
thinking explains more than the thinker's biography. Every entry carries the strongest
objections to it. Contested sources are marked contested.

## Rules any answer must satisfy

These are not style preferences. An answer that breaks them is unusable and will be thrown
away.

1. **Every claim traces to a source someone can check.** Name the archive, the publication,
   the report, the docket number or the document. "It is widely reported" is not a source. An
   encyclopaedia entry is a starting point, not a source: cite what it cites.
2. **Say UNKNOWN.** If you cannot establish the funder, the date or the institution, write
   UNKNOWN. Do not estimate, do not infer, do not reach for a plausible-sounding number.
   Never invent a date, an institution, a funder or a quotation. An answer with three solid
   cases beats twelve with invented specifics, and inventing one destroys the value of the
   other eleven.
3. **Distinguish structural tendency from conscious conspiracy.** Incentives producing an
   outcome is different from named people meeting and agreeing. The first is usually true and
   more interesting. The second is usually false and always more tempting. Where you claim
   coordination, show the minutes, the contract or the correspondence.
4. **Distinguish what a source established from what it asserted.** Where a claim is
   contested, say who contests it and on what grounds. Include the strongest objection to your
   own case.
5. **North Atlantic by default, and say so.** This round is deliberately US, UK and Western
   European, where the archives are open. Where a case has a non-Atlantic precedent or
   parallel, note it in one line rather than dropping it.
6. **House style.** Plain hyphens, no em dashes. Short sentences. No maritime metaphors
   (nothing navigating, steering, charting, anchoring, harbours, voyages, waters, on board).
   Never write "it is important to note" or any variant of "they don't want you to know". The
   material is strong enough without that register, and that register is what discredits it.
7. **Return data, not an essay.** One code block per track, in the shape specified. Prose
   commentary goes after the block, briefly, or not at all.

## Track A. Real-world manifestations

**The question.** Where is each mechanism documented as actually operating, in a named
institution, on named people, with records that survive?

For each of the following, find cases. Cover as many as you can, and mark the mechanisms where
you found nothing solid, because that absence is itself a finding.

- Credentials laundering inherited advantage into merit (Bourdieu)
- Consent manufactured through civil society rather than force (Gramsci)
- Emergency powers taken temporarily and kept (Schmitt, Agamben)
- Continuous low-level measurement producing compliant behaviour (Foucault)
- An institution past a threshold producing the opposite of its promise (Illich)
- One mode of provision abolishing the alternatives (Illich, radical monopoly)
- Legibility destroying the local knowledge it could not count (Scott)
- A metric becoming a target and ceasing to measure anything (Goodhart)
- Patronage setting the range of the sayable without censorship (the CCF model)
- Control by continuous modulation rather than enclosure (Deleuze)

**What a good answer looks like.** The Congress for Cultural Freedom is the model, and it is
already on the site: an institution with a name, a funder that took twenty years to surface,
offices in 35 countries, four magazines you can still read, a date it was exposed, participants
who dispute the interpretation, and a standard account that is itself contested. Everything is
checkable and nothing requires believing anyone lied.

Other shapes of a good answer: a school district's tracking policy with the enrolment data and
the litigation that followed. A welfare eligibility algorithm with the procurement contract,
the error rate and the tribunal decisions. An emergency power with the statute, the sunset
clause and the record of its renewals. A hospital target with the inspection report showing
what staff did to hit it.

**What a bad answer looks like.**

- "Social media manipulates people." No institution, no date, no document.
- "Foucault's panopticon is everywhere today." A restatement of theory.
- "Studies show that standardized testing reinforces inequality." Which studies, by whom, in
  what year, with what sample?
- A famous anecdote with no primary source under it.
- A case where the only evidence is that somebody powerful benefited.

**Return this shape.**

```ts
interface Case {
  slug: string;                  // kebab-case, unique
  title: string;                 // the case as a reader would name it
  years: string;                 // "1996 to 2004", or "2018", UNKNOWN if unclear
  location: string;              // country and city or jurisdiction
  institution: string;           // who ran it
  funder: string;                // who paid, or UNKNOWN
  mechanisms: string[];          // site slugs: bourdieu, gramsci, schmitt, foucault,
                                 // illich, agamben, education, media, law, psychology,
                                 // bureaucracy, scott, deleuze, zuboff, and so on
  claim: string;                 // one sentence: what this case demonstrates
  whatHappened: string;          // three sentences, plain language, no theory
  howItWorked: string;           // the mechanism in this specific case, concretely
  whoBoreIt: string;             // who was on the receiving end, and how many
  strongestObjection: string;    // the best argument that this is not what it looks like
  status: 'documented' | 'partially documented' | 'disputed';
  sources: Source[];             // at least two, at least one primary
}

interface Source {
  author?: string;
  title: string;
  year?: string;
  kind: 'archive' | 'court record' | 'government report' | 'company document'
      | 'investigation' | 'peer-reviewed' | 'book' | 'dataset';
  where: string;                 // archive and collection, publication, docket, DOI or URL
}
```

## Track B. Where the public is most exposed

**The question.** Where, concretely, is an ordinary person in a rich democracy most exposed to
these methods right now, and what can they actually do about it?

Work through the arenas rather than the theory: school and university admission, hiring and
recruitment screening, credit scoring, insurance pricing, welfare and disability eligibility,
health systems, housing and tenant screening, platform and gig work, immigration and border
control, policing and pretrial risk assessment, workplace monitoring, and the systems that
manage attention.

For each: who is exposed, at what point in a life, how the mechanism works there, what the
evidence is, how big it is with a sourced number, and what recourse exists in practice rather
than on paper.

**Rank them.** The site needs to be able to say where the exposure is worst and why. Order the
arenas by a stated criterion: how many people it touches, how hard it is to see, how little
recourse there is, or how much of a life it decides. Say which criterion you used.

**What a good answer looks like.** Automated welfare eligibility, with the state, the vendor,
the contract value, the false-negative rate from an audit, the number of households cut off,
the appeal success rate, and the fact that appeal success rates that high tell you the scoring
was wrong rather than that appeals work.

**What a bad answer looks like.** "Algorithms are biased." "Young people are addicted to their
phones." Either might be true and neither is a finding.

**Return this shape.**

```ts
interface Exposure {
  slug: string;
  arena: string;                 // "welfare eligibility", "tenant screening"
  whoIsExposed: string;          // the specific population, not "everyone"
  lifeStage: string;             // when it hits: applying to school, first job, illness
  mechanisms: string[];          // site slugs, as in Track A
  howItWorks: string;            // three sentences
  scale: string;                 // a number, with the source that produced it
  visibility: 'invisible to the person' | 'visible but unexplained' | 'disclosed';
  recourse: string;              // what a person can actually do, and what it costs them
  rank: number;                  // your ordering
  rankCriterion: string;         // the criterion you ranked by
  sources: Source[];
}
```

## Track C. Where these people actually worked, and who paid them

**The question.** The site is organized on the principle that who paid for the thinking
explains more than the thinker's biography. It applies that principle to twelve institutional
milieus and to almost nobody individually. Fix that.

For the people listed above, establish: the employer, the job title, the years, the funding
source for the specific work they are known for, what the institution's actual job was, and
what could not be said inside it.

Prioritize the ones where the answer is likely to be uncomfortable or surprising, and the ones
whose institutional position is load-bearing for how their work is read. Some obvious starts:
who funded the research programme, who the client was for applied work, which chair was
endowed and by whom, who sat on the board, and who paid for the conference series.

**What a good answer looks like.** Lazarsfeld is already on the site as the model: a bureau
funded by the Rockefeller Foundation, measuring radio audiences for sponsors who wanted
countable results, which is why the collaboration with Adorno failed inside two years and why
the discipline turned out the way it did. Employer, funder, the institution's actual job, and
the boundary of the sayable, all four.

**What a bad answer looks like.** A CV. Dates of birth and death, universities attended, prizes
won. Nothing about money.

**Return this shape.**

```ts
interface Position {
  name: string;                  // as it appears in the roster, surname first
  employer: string;
  role: string;
  years: string;
  funder: string;                // for the specific work, or UNKNOWN
  institutionsJob: string;       // what the institution actually existed to do
  couldNotBeSaid: string;        // the boundary inside that institution, or UNKNOWN
  whyItMatters: string;          // one sentence: how this changes the reading
  sources: Source[];
}
```

## Track D. Distribution channels

**The question.** Two different questions that must not be blurred.

**D1. How did the ideas travel?** Magazines, foundations, translation subsidies, prize juries,
conference circuits, university syllabi, paperback series, documentary films, and now
platforms and algorithmic recommendation. Who operated the channel, who funded it, what moved
through it, and how far it reached.

**D2. How are the techniques deployed?** The practitioners in this material were not
theorists. Bernays ran an agency with clients. Lazarsfeld ran a bureau with sponsors. The
behavioural units ran trials for governments. Find the channels: PR agencies and their client
lists, advertising networks, focus groups and panels, direct mail, broadcast, nudge units,
recommender systems, ad tech auctions, employer monitoring software, credit bureaus, data
brokers.

For both: name the operator, the money, the mechanism of reach, and the audience actually
reached with a sourced figure rather than a claimed one.

**What a good answer looks like.** *Encounter* magazine: operator, covert funder, circulation,
the writers it published, the topics it made well paid, and the year the funding surfaced. Or
a data broker: the company, the number of profiles, the categories sold, the buyers, and the
regulatory action that revealed all of it.

**What a bad answer looks like.** "Ideas spread through universities and the media."

**Return this shape.**

```ts
interface Channel {
  slug: string;
  name: string;
  side: 'ideas' | 'techniques';
  kind: 'publication' | 'foundation' | 'conference' | 'syllabus' | 'broadcast'
      | 'film' | 'agency' | 'consultancy' | 'platform' | 'data broker' | 'government unit';
  operator: string;
  funder: string;                // or UNKNOWN
  years: string;
  whatMoved: string;             // what actually travelled through it
  reach: string;                 // circulation, users, contracts, with the source
  whoItReached: string;          // which public, specifically
  sources: Source[];
}
```

## Track E. Where this is going, 2020 to 2026

**The question.** What is live now, named, and documented. Systems in deployment, laws in

Candidate areas, and add your own: algorithmic management of work, digital identity schemes,

## How to return results

- One code block per track. Valid TypeScript or JSON, matching the shape given.
- No prose wrapper, no preamble, no summary of the theory.
- Sources name an archive, a publication, a docket or a document. Not a search engine.
- Where you found nothing solid for a mechanism, arena or person, return an entry with
  `status: 'unresolved'` and one line on where you looked. Absence is a finding and it stops
  the same ground being searched twice.
- Volume: aim for 12 to 20 solid entries per track on a first pass. Three checkable cases are
  worth more than twenty thin ones.

## Six problems the site has already recorded as unsolved

If a track runs dry, these are open and worth the time. They are published on the site as
invitations rather than conclusions.

1. Map the personnel flow between cybernetics, communications research and psychological
   warfare, 1946 to 1960. Christopher Simpson does part of it in *Science of Coercion* and
   says himself it is incomplete.
2. Redo Tarde's public, which he defined by many people reading the same newspaper on the same
   morning, for an audience reading different feeds at different times.
3. Apply Illich's radical monopoly to platforms properly. Antitrust asks whether a firm
   excluded competitors. Radical monopoly asks whether a mode of provision abolished the
   alternatives, which is the more relevant question when there is no longer a non-platform
   way to do the thing.
4. Compare patronage control then and now: the Congress for Cultural Freedom model against
   research funding, philanthropic media grants and platform monetization.
5. The colonial angle, which is the largest gap. The argument that these techniques were often
   developed at the periphery and reimported.
6. Gramsci against Michels. Gramsci says build counter-hegemonic organizations. Michels says
   organization itself produces an oligarchy with interests distinct from its members, and he
   derived it from the most internally democratic party of its day. Both cannot be fully right.
