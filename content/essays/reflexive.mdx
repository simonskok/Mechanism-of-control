The [method page](/method) says to ask who paid, what the institution's job was, what
crisis was in the room, and what could not be said there. A site that puts those questions
to everyone else and not to itself has already failed them.

So here are the answers, including the ones that are not flattering. This page exists to
be updated when they change.

## Who paid, and what did they want

Nobody paid. There is no grant, no institution, no sponsor, no advertising, and no
revenue. The site was built by one person with an AI assistant, and the running costs are
a domain and a free hosting tier.

That is a real answer to the first question and it is not an unambiguously good one. No
funder means no leash, and it also means no reviewer, no editorial board, no specialist
who would have caught an error, and nobody whose reputation is at stake if this is wrong.
Unfunded is not the same as disinterested.

What the maker wanted is the honest version of the fourth question: an argument that
young people are not stupid or lazy but are inside arrangements built before they arrived.
That is a motive. It will have shaped what got included.

## The administrative and critical split, applied here

Paul Lazarsfeld named the difference between research done for an institution to improve
its operation and research asking what the institution is for.
[The Radio Project](/milieus/radio-project) is on this site because that split is the
cleanest case of funding determining findings anywhere in the material.

By that test this site is critical research, which sounds like the flattering half.
Lazarsfeld's actual point was subtler. Administrative research had sponsors who wanted
countable results, and it therefore produced them: panel studies, the two-step flow,
findings that survive. Critical research had no such discipline, and Adorno's music
section produced very little that could be checked by anyone else.

The risk here is that half. Nothing on this site has been peer reviewed, replicated, or
audited by anyone with a stake in catching it out. The only defence is that every claim
names a source and the objections are printed on the same page, which is weaker than
review and better than nothing.

## Patronage, applied here

The [Congress for Cultural Freedom](/milieus/congress-for-cultural-freedom) is the site's
own proof that control works through funding rather than censorship, and that honest
writers can produce exactly what a patron wanted without being told to.

The contemporary version of that mechanism is not the CIA. It is what gets attention.
This material has an audience that rewards a particular register: confident, totalizing,
faintly conspiratorial, flattering to the reader for having seen through things. That
register is a patron. It pays in traffic instead of money, and it selects just as
reliably.

The concrete defences are the ones that cost something. No analytics that optimize for
time on page or scroll depth. No engagement metrics of any kind. The
[Agamben COVID example](/thinkers/agamben) is published rather than quietly dropped,
because it is the site's strongest framework failing. Contested sources stay flagged as
contested even when they are the most quotable.

## Goodhart, applied here

A measure that becomes a target stops measuring anything.

If this site optimized for readers, pages, time on page, or shares, those numbers would go
up and the quality of the thing would go down, because everything on this page's list of
defences costs attention. That is not a hypothetical about someone else. It is the
mechanism the site documents, pointed at the site, and the only reliable protection is not
to collect the numbers.

So it does not. There is no analytics on these pages. The cost is real: without them
nobody knows which pages work, and the site is flying on judgement.

## Michels, applied here

Robert Michels derived the [iron law of oligarchy](/figures/michels) from the most
internally democratic mass party of its day: organization itself produces a leadership
stratum with interests distinct from the members.

One person and an AI is not an organization, so the law does not bite yet. It would apply
the moment this became a group with contributors, an editorial line, and a reputation to
defend, and the first thing an editorial line defends is itself. The site has no answer
to this. It is recorded on [open questions](/open-questions) as an unresolved conflict in
the material, and it is unresolved here for the same reason.

## What could not be said here

The honest answer is that the boundary has not been tested, because nothing has been at
stake yet. That is not a claim of freedom, it is an absence of pressure.

Two things are visible already. The site is North Atlantic by default, and the colonial
argument that many of these techniques were developed at the periphery and imported back
would produce a different site, which is recorded as the largest gap rather than filled.
And the material is heavily weighted toward critics of existing arrangements, because
those are the people who wrote it down. The strongest defences of the same institutions
are underrepresented, and that is a real distortion rather than a neutral choice.

## The failure mode this page exists to prevent

Not despair. Superiority.

This material is very good at producing readers who feel they have seen through
everything and now hold a position over people who have not. That is corrosive, it
isolates, and it is the on-ramp to the conspiratorial register the
[method page](/method) rules out.

Pierre Bourdieu's own discipline is the antidote, and it is the reason this page exists:
turn the analysis on your own position first. A reader who takes anything from this site
should be able to run the four questions on it and get real answers back.

If something on this page is wrong, it is wrong in the direction of the maker's interest.
That is the general case, and there is no reason to think it does not apply here.
