This page is mostly a map of what a project built from this research is not, and it is here
because the question of who pays is the first question this material puts to any source,
including itself.

## What already exists

There is a European template for a youth think tank, and it works.

**foraus**, founded in Switzerland in 2009, is the model most of the others copy.
Participatory, non-partisan, volunteer-run, with regional branches in university cities, an
elected board and a general assembly, and roughly a thousand members. Its founder, Nicola
Forster, an Ashoka Fellow, built it on the observation that young people are interested in
issue politics rather than party politics, which is still the correct observation.

foraus also developed **Policy Kitchen**, a crowdsourcing method that pairs a digital platform
with physical workshops of roughly ten to forty people. Worth studying as a method regardless
of what you think of the output.

**Polis180**, founded in Berlin in 2015, has over 650 members, thematic programmes and a
Beyond Berlin remote track, funded by donations, membership fees and contributions.

**Argo** in Paris, **Ponto** and others are federated in the **Open Think Tank Network**.
Alongside them sit **ThinkYoung** in Brussels, founded in 2009, the **European Student Think
Tank**, the **European Youth Think Tank**, **On Think Tanks** with its Building for the Future
initiative, and the **European Youth Forum**.

## The funding on offer

- **CERV**, the Citizens, Equality, Rights and Values programme, funds civil society at
  roughly 90 per cent rates for transnational democracy and participation projects.
- **Erasmus+** and **European Youth Together** fund youth participation and network building.
- National foundations and party-affiliated foundations.
- Municipal support, usually in the form of space.

One caveat on the record, and it matters for anyone planning around this: think tanks have
been increasingly deprioritized in EU funding, and the 2026 to 2028 CERV operating grant
results showed little support for them.

## Why this route is structurally closed

An organization whose subject is how institutions manufacture legitimacy cannot take its
money from those institutions.

This is not a purity argument and it is not an accusation against anyone taking the grants.
It is the [first failure mode](/method) stated as an operating constraint. Grant dependence
plus access dependence selects, over time and without anyone intending it, for criticism that
never threatens the source. Nobody has to be told what to conclude. The proposals that fit
get funded, the people who write them get the next post, and everyone involved is doing
honest work.

[The Congress for Cultural Freedom](/milieus/congress-for-cultural-freedom) is what that looks
like when it works, and it worked on writers who did not know where the money came from and
who insisted afterwards that it had changed nothing they wrote. Some of them were probably
right. It changed which things existed.

## The model that preserves independence

Audience revenue. Membership dues, patronage, paid seminars and reading-intensive courses, a
serious publication, and books.

The form that implies is closer to an institute plus a journal plus a salon, a reading and
research order, than to a policy NGO or an advocacy organization. It is slower, smaller and
harder to start. It answers to readers rather than to programme officers, which is the entire
point.

## Design principles

- Non-partisanship written into the statutes rather than into the marketing.
- Federated local chapters rather than a central headquarters.
- Low-barrier entry followed by rigorous output.
- Senior review pairing for every junior author.
- A registered non-profit association with an elected board.
- Quality control as the actual moat.

## The standing objection

[Michels](/figures/michels) derived the iron law of oligarchy from exactly this kind of
organization: a membership body, internally democratic, run by people who meant it. His
finding was that the leadership stratum forms anyway, and acquires interests distinct from
the members, through the ordinary requirements of running anything.

Any organization built out of this research has to answer that objection about itself, and
the honest position is that nobody has answered it yet. It is in the
[open questions](/open-questions).
