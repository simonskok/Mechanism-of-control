Nothing here is long except by choice. Four of the seven below are essays, and two of
those are under twenty pages.

## First seven, for a newcomer with no background

1. **C. Wright Mills, *The Sociological Imagination*, chapter 1.** The move this entire
   site is built on: personal troubles into public issues.
2. **Václav Havel, *The Power of the Powerless*.** Essay length. The greengrocer, and the
   best single text on control that runs without belief. Discussed on the
   [East and West page](/east-west).
3. **Ivan Illich, *Deschooling Society*.** Short, furious, and the fastest route to
   [radical monopoly](/thinkers/illich).
4. **Michel Foucault, *Discipline and Punish*, Part Three, "Panopticism".** One chapter.
   Read the chapter before you decide whether to read the book.
5. **Gilles Deleuze, "Postscript on the Societies of Control".** Six pages. Read it
   immediately after Foucault, in the same week, or it loses half its force.
6. **Pierre Bourdieu, "The Forms of Capital".** Essay length, and the clearest statement
   of [the school argument](/thinkers/bourdieu).
7. **Herman and Chomsky, *Manufacturing Consent*, introduction and chapter 1.** The five
   filters, in the authors' own words rather than in summary.

## Extension path, if you already have the core

1. **Deleuze, "Postscript"** again, properly this time. It reframes everything written
   about discipline before it.
2. **Norbert Wiener, *The Human Use of Human Beings*.** Supplies the mechanism underneath
   the philosophy. See [the Macy Conferences](/milieus/macy-conferences).
3. **Lazarsfeld's 1941 essay on administrative and critical research**, then Christopher
   Simpson, *Science of Coercion*. How the funding structure of a field determines its
   findings. See [the Radio Project](/milieus/radio-project).
4. **Harold Innis, *The Bias of Communication*.** The materialist base for everything
   about media.
5. **Michael Mann on infrastructural power**, then **Timothy Mitchell, *Carbon
   Democracy***. Moves the analysis from ideas to pipes. See
   [infrastructural power](/milieus/infrastructural-power).
6. **Illich, *Tools for Conviviality*.** The only text in this project oriented to
   building rather than diagnosing.
7. **La Boétie, *Discourse on Voluntary Servitude*.** Short, and the oldest statement of
   the central question.

## Journals and outlets

*Theory, Culture & Society*. *Critical Inquiry*. *New Left Review*. *Radical Philosophy*.
*Journal of Political Ideologies*. *Constellations*. *Eurozine*, which is essential for
Central and Eastern European debate and is multilingual. *Aeon*. *Boston Review*.

## Archives and open resources

Marxists Internet Archive, for Gramsci. UbuWeb and Monoskop, for theory and film. Open
Library. Foucault.info. Illich's collected works at The Preserve, davidtinapple.com/illich.
The Internet Archive for out-of-print material.

## Film and documentary

**Adam Curtis**, with the caveat on [the media page](/domains/media) attached: watch, then
check what moved you. *The Century of the Self* is the one to start with.

*The Corporation* (2003). Godard's *La Chinoise* and *Tout va bien* as period artifacts
rather than arguments. *The Lives of Others* (2006) for the Eastern surveillance state.
*Videocracy* (2009) on Berlusconi's media hegemony.

## Contemporary continuations

Byung-Chul Han on achievement society and self-exploitation, which is probably the
easiest way into this material if you are under forty. Wendy Brown on neoliberal subject
formation. Mark Fisher, *Capitalist Realism*, on the inability to imagine alternatives.
Ivan Krastev on Europe's east and west split. Shoshana Zuboff, with the objections
attached.

## The counter-tradition

Analysis without a constructive edge produces exactly the paralysis Agamben is accused
of. This is the other half of the material.

- **Mills.** Personal troubles into public issues. The reframing that turns private
  failure into a shared condition.
- **La Boétie.** The tyrant has only the power you lend him.
- **Freire.** Literacy taught as the reading of your own situation.
- **Gramsci.** The long build inside civil society.
- **Illich.** Conviviality, and keeping tools below the threshold at which they enforce
  dependence. The most directly usable design brief here.
- **Wiener.** A founder publicly refusing the funding that would have directed his work,
  in print, in 1947. A precedent rather than a theory.
- **Havel.** Living in truth. The greengrocer takes the sign down.
- **Scott.** Mētis and the hidden transcript. The defence of practical local knowledge
  and everyday resistance.
- **Bourdieu.** Reflexivity: turn the analysis on your own position first. This is also
  the discipline that stops the material becoming a way of feeling superior to other
  people.
