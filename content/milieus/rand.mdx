## The climate

Nuclear standoff. The central problems could not be experimented on, so they had to be
modelled instead, and that put enormous authority into formalism: game theory,
operations research, linear programming, systems analysis.

A model is not a lie. It is a set of assumptions that has to be explicit enough to
compute. The trouble starts when the assumptions travel.

## Who was in the room

John von Neumann. Albert Wohlstetter. Thomas Schelling. Herman Kahn. Kenneth Arrow.
Bernard Brodie. Later Daniel Ellsberg, whose defection makes him the best inside witness
this cluster has.

## What came out of it

Rational-choice assumptions built for deterrence migrated outward into welfare policy,
education, policing and management, carrying an implicit picture of the human being
with them: the strategic, self-interested actor. Nobody in those other fields chose that
anthropology. It arrived with the method.

Control here is not somebody watching you. It is the model deciding which options appear
on the page at all.

**Key works.** Schelling, *The Strategy of Conflict* (1960). Kahn, *On Thermonuclear
War* (1960), which is genuinely hard to read and worth the discomfort. Ellsberg,
*Secrets* (2002) and *The Doomsday Machine* (2017).

## What it adds

This is where [bureaucracy](/domains/bureaucracy) acquires its modern intellectual
authority. Weber described officials applying rules. RAND produced officials applying
models, which is harder to argue with because the argument now requires the same
mathematics.

## Institutional history

S. M. Amadae, *Rationalizing Capitalist Democracy* (2003), on how rational choice became
the default language of legitimacy. Paul Erickson and colleagues, *How Reason Almost
Lost Its Mind* (2013), on Cold War rationality as a specific historical object rather
than a timeless standard.
