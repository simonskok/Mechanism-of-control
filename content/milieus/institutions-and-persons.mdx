## Goffman

**Erving Goffman, *Asylums* (1961)**, based on fieldwork inside a psychiatric hospital,
describes the <Term id="total-institution">total institution</Term> and what he calls
the moral career of the inmate.

Admission procedures are the mechanism: the removal of clothes and possessions, the
standard issue, the number, the form, the requirement to account for yourself to
strangers. What looks like administrative necessity is a sequence that dismantles a
person's existing self and installs one the institution can work with.

The other half of the finding is the underlife: the small evasions, workarounds and
private irony through which inmates keep something back. Goffman's point is that the
underlife is part of the system rather than a failure of it. Every institution runs on
its own informal violations.

Goffman is the microphysics that pairs with [Foucault's](/thinkers/foucault)
macrophysics, and he is far easier to test against your own experience of a school, a
hospital, a barracks or an office.

Also *The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life* (1956) and *Stigma* (1963).

## Illich

Full treatment on [his own page](/thinkers/illich). His milieu belongs here. CIDOC in
Cuernavaca was funded by language-course fees rather than by foundations, which is an
unusual funding structure and directly explains what he was able to publish. He was
running an institution while arguing that its entire sector should not exist, and the
Vatican investigated him for it in 1968.

## Elias

**Norbert Elias, *The Civilizing Process* (1939)**, written in exile and ignored until
the 1970s. External restraint becomes self-restraint as state monopolies on violence and
taxation lengthen the chains of interdependence between people. Manners, disgust and
embarrassment are the visible residue of a long process of moving control from outside a
person to inside them.

It is the panopticon claim made across five centuries and with evidence, and it was
written before Foucault was born.

## What it adds

This cluster is where the argument stops being abstract. You have been in at least one
of these institutions. The question is what it issued you with, and what you kept back.
