<MetaStrip />

Ten conferences, small and interdisciplinary by design, held in New York in the years
immediately after the Second World War.

## The climate

Straight out of the war. Norbert Wiener's wartime job was an anti-aircraft predictor:
model a pilot's evasive behaviour accurately enough to put a shell where he is going to
be. The founding gesture of cybernetics is treating a human being as a predictable
feedback system under fire, and that origin never entirely washes out of the field.

## Who was in the room

Norbert Wiener and John von Neumann. Warren McCulloch in the chair, with Walter Pitts.
Claude Shannon. Gregory Bateson and Margaret Mead. Heinz von Foerster. Kurt Lewin.

Anthropologists and psychiatrists sitting with mathematicians and engineers, which is
exactly why the vocabulary of feedback, homeostasis and information moved into the
social sciences as fast as it did.

## What came out of it

**Wiener, *Cybernetics* (1948)** and, more usefully, **The Human Use of Human Beings
(1950, substantially revised 1954)**. The title has two edges. Wiener built the science
of control through feedback and then wrote the warning: a society that treats people as
components in a control loop, managed by information they do not hold, degrades them
into messages in somebody else's system. Read the 1950 and 1954 editions against each
other. The second is more pessimistic and the changes are informative.

Wiener also refused military funding after 1947 and published the refusal, as "A
Scientist Rebels" in the *Atlantic*, January 1947. A founder trying to withdraw a
technology from the institution that paid for it is rare enough that the document is
worth reading in full, and it is the reason he appears in the
[counter-tradition](/method).

**Bateson, *Steps to an Ecology of Mind* (1972)** applied feedback to families, schools
and schizophrenia, and produced the double bind: a control structure with no exit, where
every available response is punished.

**Heinz von Foerster** developed second-order cybernetics, whose point is that the
observer is inside the system being observed. Anyone building a theory of control should
sit with that one for a while.

## What it adds

The engineering layer underneath the philosophy. [Foucault](/thinkers/foucault)
describes discipline; cybernetics supplies the actual mechanism, feedback and the servo
loop, plus a first-generation insider's alarm about applying it to people.

The line runs directly from here to
[control after discipline](/milieus/control-after-discipline), where Deleuze describes
control as continuous modulation, which is a feedback loop by another name.

## Institutional history

Steve Joshua Heims, *The Cybernetics Group* (1991). Ronald Kline, *The Cybernetics
Moment* (2015). Both are institutional accounts rather than intellectual biographies,
which is the right way to read this cluster.
