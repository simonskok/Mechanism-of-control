## La Boétie, and the question underneath everything

**Étienne de La Boétie, *Discourse on Voluntary Servitude***, written around 1548 when
he was about eighteen and circulated in manuscript.

The question is not why tyrants want to rule. It is why millions obey a man who has only
the power they lend him. Stop lending it and he falls, with no violence required at all.

Everything from [Gramsci](/thinkers/gramsci) to Gene Sharp is downstream of this, and
[Havel's greengrocer](/east-west) is the twentieth-century restatement.

## Tarde against Le Bon

**Gabriel Tarde, *L'opinion et la foule* (1901)**, argued against Le Bon that the modern
formation is not the physical crowd but the **public**: dispersed, never assembled, held
together only by simultaneous reading of the same newspaper.

He identified the mediated public decades before broadcast, and network theory has
substantially rehabilitated him. What his mechanism does under recommender systems, where
people read different feeds at different times, is one of the
[open questions](/open-questions).

<Contested status="Included for its effect, not its truth">
**Gustave Le Bon, *The Crowd* (1895)** is weak as science and historically decisive. It
was read by Mussolini, by Hitler, and by the founders of public relations. It belongs
here because of what it did, not because of what it established, and reading it that way
is a useful exercise in itself.
</Contested>

## The technician's account

**Serge Chakhotin, *The Rape of the Masses* (1939).** A student of Pavlov who worked on
anti-Nazi symbol campaigns for the German Social Democrats and analysed propaganda as
conditioned reflex. A rare thing: a technical account written by someone using the
techniques defensively, and aware of the problem with that.

## Veblen

**Thorstein Veblen**, *The Theory of the Leisure Class* (1899) and *The Engineers and
the Price System* (1921). Conspicuous consumption as a status control mechanism, sabotage
as a normal business practice rather than a scandal, and the rule of a technical class as
a live proposal rather than a warning.
