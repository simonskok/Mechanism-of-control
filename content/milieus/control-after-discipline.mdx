## The insight

**Gilles Deleuze, "Postscript on the Societies of Control" (1990)** is six pages and the
highest-yield short text in this whole project.

Discipline operated by enclosure and by moulds. You passed from the family to the
school to the barracks to the factory, each one a separate casting, each with a
threshold you crossed and a point at which you were finished with it.

Control operates by <Term id="modulation">modulation</Term>: continuous assessment,
perpetual training, rolling credit, no graduation and no discharge. The factory was a
body. The corporation is a gas. The signature of discipline is the signature and the
file. The signature of control is the password and the code. The individual becomes a
<Term id="dividual">dividual</Term>, split into data attributes and scored in pieces.

He wrote it before the web, and it is unnervingly accurate about it.

## Follow-ons worth the time

- **Nikolas Rose**, *Governing the Soul* (1989) and *Powers of Freedom* (1999), on how
  psychology as a discipline made the self governable, and how advanced liberal rule
  works through freedom, choice and self-management rather than against them. Rose and
  Peter Miller, "Political Power beyond the State" (1992), is the compact statement.
  This is the most useful extension of [Foucault's](/thinkers/foucault) governmentality.
- **Wendy Brown**, *Undoing the Demos* (2015), on the conversion of citizens into human
  capital and of political questions into questions of competitiveness.
- **Achille Mbembe**, "Necropolitics" (2003), on what biopower looks like from the
  colony rather than the metropole, where the power in question is the power to expose
  to death. These techniques were frequently developed at the periphery and reimported,
  which is the largest gap in this project and is recorded in the
  [open questions](/open-questions).
- **Byung-Chul Han**, *The Burnout Society* (2010) and *Psychopolitics* (2014). The
  shift from a disciplinary society to an
  <Term id="achievement-society">achievement society</Term>, where the subject exploits
  itself voluntarily and experiences the exploitation as freedom. If one text in this
  project is going to land immediately with a reader under forty, it is this one.

## What it adds

It updates the whole framework. Read *Discipline and Punish*, then read the Deleuze
essay the same week rather than two years later, and the argument arrives in the present
tense: the continuous assessment, the score, the training that never ends, the
subscription that never lapses.
