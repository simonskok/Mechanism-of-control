## The climate

Defeat. In 1947 this was a marginal group convinced that the postwar consensus was
permanent and that they had lost. The interesting question is not the doctrine. It is
how a marginal network became the default administrative language of states within four
decades.

## What it adds, and why it is handled carefully here

This is the one cluster where the conspiracy-shaped reading is partly correct.
Coordination is documented: there are meeting minutes, funding structures and personnel
flows that can be read. That contradicts any assumption that hegemony is always
impersonal drift.

It is also the reason this cluster needs the strictest evidentiary standard on the site
rather than the loosest. Coordination is documented. Omnipotence is not. A network that
spent forty years building institutions is not the same thing as a network that controls
outcomes, and treating the two as identical is the fastest route to the second failure
mode on the [method page](/method).

Read next to [Gramsci](/thinkers/gramsci): this is a successful
<Term id="war-of-position">war of position</Term> conducted from the right, and it is
the best empirical test his strategy has ever had.

## Key works

- Philip Mirowski and Dieter Plehwe (eds.), *The Road from Mont Pèlerin* (2009). The
  archival collection.
- Mirowski, *Never Let a Serious Crisis Go to Waste* (2013). His central argument is
  that the movement's distinctive achievement was using state power to build and defend
  markets while speaking a language of state retreat.
- Quinn Slobodian, *Globalists* (2018), on the Geneva School designing supranational
  insulation of markets from democratic majorities. This is the most relevant strand for
  this site: control achieved by removing questions from the domain of decision
  altogether.
- Angus Burgin, *The Great Persuasion* (2012).

## The counterpoint, and it is essential

Karl Polanyi, *The Great Transformation* (1944). The self-regulating market was itself
an act of state construction rather than a natural condition that states interfered
with. Written before the Mont Pelerin meeting and answering it in advance.

<Contested status="Vigorously contested">
Nancy MacLean, *Democracy in Chains* (2017), on James Buchanan and public choice theory,
is disputed by historians and economists on questions of quotation and inference. If you
use it, use it with the rebuttals in hand. The stronger version of the argument does not
need it.
</Contested>
