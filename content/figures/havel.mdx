If you read one thing from this site, read the essay this page is about. It is short, it was
written by someone who paid for writing it, and its central image will stay with you.

<Thesis source="The Power of the Powerless, 1978">
The greengrocer puts the sign in his window. He does not believe it, nobody thinks he does,
and nobody reads it. The sign says: I am obedient, and therefore I have the right to be left
alone.
</Thesis>

## The greengrocer

A manager of a fruit and vegetable shop places a placard among the onions and carrots.
Workers of the world, unite.

Ask what the sign is for and the official answer, that he supports the cause, is obviously
false. Havel's answer is that the sign is a signal of compliance addressed to the people who
check, and its content is irrelevant. If the placard read "I am afraid and therefore
unquestioningly obedient", the greengrocer would be ashamed to display it, and that shame is
exactly what the ideological wording exists to prevent.

## Post-totalitarianism

Havel calls the system <Term id="post-totalitarianism">post-totalitarian</Term> not because
it came after totalitarianism but because it no longer requires belief. It runs on
<Term id="living-within-a-lie">living within a lie</Term>: everyone performs assent, and each
performance is part of the pressure on everyone else.

The consequence is that there is no clean division between victims and enforcers. The
greengrocer is both. So is the person who sees his sign and puts up their own.

The counter-move is living in truth, which means refusing the ritual. Taking the sign down is
not a political programme and does not overthrow anything. It is dangerous because it shows
the others that the sign was always a choice.

## Why it matters for the theory

Havel describes hegemony without belief, and that is the limit case both
[Gramsci](/thinkers/gramsci) and [Bourdieu](/thinkers/bourdieu) have to survive. Their
mechanisms assume people internalize the legitimating story. Havel's greengrocer internalized
nothing and complied anyway.

<Objections>
- It describes a specific system at a specific moment. Applying post-totalitarianism to
  consumer democracies, as people routinely do, drops the part where refusal costs you your
  job, your children's education and sometimes your liberty.
- Living in truth is a moral stance rather than a strategy, and Havel's later career as
  president is a fair place to test what happened when it met governing.
- The essay's power is literary, which makes it persuasive in ways that are hard to check
  against evidence.
</Objections>
