<Thesis source="The Mind and Society, 1916">
Derivations are the reasons people give. Residues are the drives underneath. Study the
residues.
</Thesis>

## The argument

Pareto separates what people say they are doing from what is actually moving them. The
justifications, which he calls derivations, are endlessly variable and mostly rationalization.
The underlying drives, the residues, are few and stable.

From that he gets the circulation of elites: ruling groups do not improve or reform so much
as get replaced, one set of residues giving way to another, while the derivations are rewritten
to match. The formula changes, the structure does not.

## Where it sits

Read next to [Mosca](/figures/mosca), who is describing the same phenomenon with more
attention to institutions, and next to the psychology of
[system justification](/domains/psychology), which is the experimental version of the claim
that people generate reasons for arrangements they did not choose.

<Objections>
- The residues are not measurable in any agreed way, which makes the framework
  unfalsifiable in practice.
- Dismissing stated reasons as decoration is a move that can be applied to any argument
  including Pareto's own, and it corrodes the possibility of taking anyone seriously.
- His work was enthusiastically claimed by Italian fascism. He died in 1923, too early for
  the full record, but the affinity is not accidental and should not be brushed off.
</Objections>
