<Thesis source="Governing the Soul, 1989, and Powers of Freedom, 1999">
Modern government does not need to suppress your freedom. It works through it, by shaping
what a free person wants and how they manage themselves.
</Thesis>

## The argument

Rose takes [Foucault's](/thinkers/foucault)
<Term id="governmentality">governmentality</Term> and does the historical work on one case:
the psychological professions.

Psychology supplied the vocabulary in which a person can be assessed, compared, and improved:
aptitude, adjustment, motivation, personality, potential. It supplied the instruments for
doing it at scale, from the aptitude test to the appraisal. And it supplied the idea that
each of us has an inner life that is our responsibility to work on.

The result is a citizen who governs themselves in the terms the institutions need, and
experiences it as self-development. Nobody has to be coerced into an annual performance
review. It feels like an opportunity.

## Why it lands now

The self-optimizing employee, the wellbeing app, the personal brand and the therapy language
of workplace management are all this argument in the present tense. Rose and Peter Miller's
"Political Power beyond the State" (1992) is the compact statement if you want it in twenty
pages.

Read next to [Han](/figures/han), who takes the same observation somewhere angrier, and to
[the behavioural state](/milieus/behavioural-state), which is where the technique acquired a
budget.

<Objections>
- The account gives psychology enormous causal weight. Managerial capitalism might have found
  other vocabularies for the same work.
- It can flatten a real difference between a therapy that helps someone and an appraisal
  system that manages them, because both use the language of the self.
- Like most work in this line, it explains how the arrangement holds far better than it
  explains how anyone resists it.
</Objections>
