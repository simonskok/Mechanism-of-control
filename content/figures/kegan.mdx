Kegan supplies the mechanism for why reading this site can feel destabilizing rather than
informative, and why that feeling is not a malfunction.

<Thesis source="The Evolving Self, 1982, and In Over Our Heads, 1994">
What you are embedded in, you cannot see. Development is the process by which what you were
becomes something you have.
</Thesis>

## The subject-object shift

Kegan's central move is a distinction between what is subject and what is object in a person's
mental life.

Subject is what you are fused with. You cannot reflect on it, because you are looking through
it rather than at it. Object is what you can hold at a distance, examine, argue with, and
decide about.

The <Term id="subject-object-shift">subject-object shift</Term> is the conversion of the first
into the second. A teenager who is their feelings becomes an adult who has feelings and can
consider them. Someone who is their loyalty to a group becomes someone who holds that loyalty
and can weigh it.

Every page on this site is an attempt at one of these shifts. Your
<Term id="habitus">habitus</Term> was subject, it felt like personality, and the point of the
Bourdieu page is to make it object. The same is true of
<Term id="common-sense">common sense</Term>, of <Term id="modulation">modulation</Term>, and of
the assumption that the assessment ends.

## Why it costs something

If what you could not see was also what you were, then seeing it is a loss before it is a
gain. Kegan is explicit that these transitions involve real grief and a period where the old
way has gone and the new one has not arrived.

His broader argument in *In Over Our Heads* is that modern life makes demands that exceed the
developmental capacity most adults have, and that the result is a chronic sense of being
overwhelmed by tasks that look, from outside, like ordinary adulthood.

## What it implies for this site

Two things. That the discomfort of these pages is the process rather than a sign the pages are
badly written. And that a reader cannot be argued into a shift, because you cannot reason your
way out of something you are currently looking through. What helps is a supportive holding
environment, in Kegan's term: enough challenge to force the shift, enough support to survive
it.

That is a design brief, and it is the reason this site pairs difficulty with something
usable rather than delivering difficulty alone.

<Objections>
- Stage theories imply that later is better and that everyone travels the same road. Both
  claims have been criticized as culturally specific, with the endpoint looking a great deal
  like an educated Western professional.
- The empirical base is small. Assessment relies on a lengthy subject-object interview scored
  by trained raters, and the sample sizes behind the stage claims are modest.
- The theory is widely used in corporate leadership development, where it functions as a
  ranking of people. That use is worth noticing given what the rest of this site says about
  measurement.
</Objections>
