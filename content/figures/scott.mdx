<Thesis source="Seeing Like a State, 1998">
Legibility is the state's project. The simplification is not neutral, because what cannot be
counted gets destroyed rather than merely overlooked.
</Thesis>

## Legibility

A state that wants to tax, conscript or police has to be able to read its population. So it
builds instruments: cadastral surveys, permanent surnames, standard weights and measures,
grid cities, censuses.

Scott's point is that <Term id="legibility">legibility</Term> does not just describe. The
scientifically managed forest is his opening case: German foresters replaced a messy woodland
with straight rows of a single species because it could be counted and its yield predicted.
The first generation grew well. The second collapsed, because the countable forest had
removed the undergrowth, fungi, deadwood and species mix the trees were living on. There is a
German word for what followed: *Waldsterben*, forest death.

Combine legibility with high-modernist confidence, authoritarian power and a population that
cannot resist, and you get the catastrophes the book anatomizes.

## Mētis

<Term id="metis">Mētis</Term> is his word for practical local knowledge that cannot be
written into a procedure: when the soil is ready, which patient is deteriorating, how this
particular machine behaves. It is exactly what legibility cannot capture and therefore what
it destroys.

That is the same argument [Illich](/thinkers/illich) makes about disabling professions,
arrived at from the state rather than from the client.

## The hidden transcript

*Domination and the Arts of Resistance* (1990) adds the public transcript, the performance
subordinates give in front of power, and the
<Term id="hidden-transcript">hidden transcript</Term>, what they actually say behind it.

This is the strongest available answer to the objection that
[Bourdieu](/thinkers/bourdieu) and [Foucault](/thinkers/foucault) leave people no room to
act. Scott's subordinates are not dupes. They are performing, and they know they are.

<Objections>
- The state is not the only simplifier. Corporations, platforms and NGOs do the same thing,
  and the book's framing lets market-driven legibility off lightly.
- The high-modernist cases are selected for failure. Legibility also delivered vaccination,
  land reform and public health, and the book gives that side little room.
- Mētis can shade into romanticizing local knowledge, some of which is simply wrong.
</Objections>
