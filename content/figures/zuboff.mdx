<Thesis source="The Age of Surveillance Capitalism, 2019">
Behaviour is taken as raw material, refined into prediction products, and sold. Once you are
selling predictions, the shortest route to accuracy is intervention.
</Thesis>

## The mechanism

Zuboff's term is behavioural surplus: the data exhaust left over once a service has what it
needs to function. Your search returns results with a fraction of what your search reveals.
The remainder is the asset.

Refined, that surplus becomes a prediction product sold to whoever wants to know what you
will do. And the competitive pressure runs in one direction: a prediction is more valuable
the more certain it is, so the incentive is to stop forecasting behaviour and start nudging
it.

## The difference from the panopticon

[Foucault's](/thinkers/foucault) prisoner behaves because he might be watched and cannot
check. The watching does the work by being felt.

Here the watching is not felt at all. There is no tower, no uncertainty, no sense of being
observed. That is the update this project needs, and it is why
[Deleuze's](/figures/deleuze) modulation describes the present better than enclosure does.

appears with a public budget and published trials.

<Objections>
- Criticized for technological determinism, and for treating one advertising business model
  as a total civilizational rupture rather than a phase of it.
- The claim that behavioural futures markets reliably shape behaviour outruns the published
  evidence on how well targeting actually works.
- Political economists argue the book gives capitalism itself a clean bill of health by
  locating the problem in a deviant new form of it.
</Objections>
