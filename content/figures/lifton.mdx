<Thesis source="Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism, 1961">
Lifton interviewed people who had been through thought reform programmes in China and found
that the process had structure. Eight recurring features, none of them requiring force, and
none of them confined to the extreme case he studied.
</Thesis>

## Why this one is a tool and not a theory

Most of this site describes mechanisms. Lifton's eight criteria are different in kind,
because they were built to be run by a person on their own situation, and they still work
that way sixty years later.

The eight are set out in full on the [tools page](/tools), with the caveats that matter.
What follows is why they belong in the argument.

## The mechanism

Lifton's finding is that coercion does not have to look like coercion. The programmes he
studied worked by controlling what reached a person, splitting the world into pure and
impure, extracting confessions that then functioned as leverage, and shrinking the available
vocabulary until certain questions could not be formed.

That last one, which he called loading the language, is the item with the widest reach.
A <Term id="thought-terminating-cliche">thought-terminating cliche</Term> is a short phrase
that closes an argument instead of answering it. It is cheap, it feels like a point, and it
removes a question from circulation. Nothing about it requires a cult.

The criteria describe a direction, not a verdict. Most institutions show one or two of them.
The reading is in the count and in which way the count is moving.

## Where it sits in the argument

[Gramsci](/thinkers/gramsci) says consent is manufactured through the institutions of
ordinary life. Lifton supplies the mechanics at close range: not what a class does over
decades, but what happens to one person's vocabulary inside one group over months.

Next to [Foucault](/thinkers/foucault) the difference is confession. Foucault treats the
confessional as a technique that produces the self it claims to uncover. Lifton watched the
same technique used deliberately, by people who knew what it was for, and recorded the
result as damage.

[Goffman](/figures/goffman) on total institutions covers the same ground from outside, in
the language of roles and settings rather than of belief.

<Objections>
- The category of thought reform, and the popular word brainwashing that grew out of it, has
  been heavily contested. Sociologists of religion argued through the 1980s that conversion
  to new religious movements is better explained by ordinary social influence and by who
  chooses to stay, since most recruits leave of their own accord.
- The American Psychological Association episode is worth stating precisely, because it gets
  used loosely by both sides. In a memorandum of 11 May 1987 the APA's Board of Social and
  Ethical Responsibility for Psychology told the members of its Task Force on Deceptive and
  Indirect Methods of Persuasion and Control, chaired by Margaret Singer, that it was "unable
  to accept the report of the Task Force" because the report "lacks the scientific rigor and
  evenhanded critical approach necessary for APA imprimatur". The board did not ask for a
  revision, and it warned the members not to imply APA support for any later version either.
  In the same memorandum it also said it did "not believe that we have sufficient information
  available to guide us in taking a position on this issue".
  So the APA refused a report. It did not rule on whether coercive persuasion is real, and
  anyone citing this as the moment brainwashing was scientifically discredited is claiming
  something the document explicitly declines to say.

- The eight criteria are descriptive and were derived from a small set of interviews in one
  extreme setting. They have no established predictive validity and no agreed threshold, so
  the count that makes them useful is also the part with no evidence behind it.
- Because the list is easy to apply and satisfying to apply, it works as a labelling device.
  Lifton's own warning is that a checklist used to sort people becomes the thing it
  describes, and that failure is common in practice.
- Applying a model built from political imprisonment to workplaces, universities or online
  communities flattens a real difference in what is at stake and what can be refused.
</Objections>
