Bourdieu started as an ethnographer in Algeria during the war of independence, then
spent his career on French schools at the moment they were expanding. The expansion
was supposed to open the elite to everyone. It measurably did not, and explaining why
became his life's work.

<Thesis source="Reproduction in Education, Society and Culture, 1970">
The school converts inherited privilege into certified merit, and persuades the losers
that the verdict is a fact about their own nature.
</Thesis>

<Mechanism />

## Symbolic violence

Two words doing exact work.

Violence, because the damage is real: people end up in narrower lives than they had
any reason to. Symbolic, because it works through meaning and categories rather than
through force. Nobody is hit.

The part that makes it strong is that it operates
<Term id="symbolic-violence">with the agreement of the person it is done to</Term>.
A student who says "I am just not academic" has taken the institution's verdict, made
it a fact about themselves, and carried out the sentence without anyone having to
enforce it. Bourdieu's word for accepting the fairness of the standard that ranks you
low is <Term id="misrecognition">misrecognition</Term>. The violence works only
because it is not experienced as violence.

## Why giftedness is the keystone

Attributing success to natural talent rather than to transmitted advantage turns an
inequality that society produced into one that looks like nature. It is the most
efficient legitimating idea available, because it convinces the winners and the losers
at the same time and requires no enforcement at all.

Compare an open aristocracy. It is visibly unjust, everyone can see the mechanism, and
it can be argued with. A credential system produces winners who sincerely believe they
earned it, losers who believe they deserved it, and a handful of people who rose from
the bottom whose existence is offered as proof that anyone could.

Michael Young coined the word meritocracy in a 1958 satire, *The Rise of the
Meritocracy*, meaning it as a warning about exactly this. The word was then adopted as
a compliment, which is its own small case study.

## Psychological underpinning

Internalizing external evaluation until it becomes self-concept. Stereotype threat and
self-handicapping. And the just-world hypothesis, Melvin Lerner's finding that people
need to believe outcomes are deserved, which produces both victim-blaming and, more
quietly, self-blame.

## Primary works

- *The Forms of Capital* (1986). An essay, and the best short entry point by a distance.
- *Reproduction in Education, Society and Culture* (1970), with Jean-Claude Passeron.
- *The Inheritors* (1964). The study of French students that started it.
- *Distinction* (1979). Taste as a class marker, at book length.

<Objections>
- Charged with determinism. If habitus is absorbed early and runs below conscious
  choice, it is unclear where change or resistance would come from.
- The empirical size of cultural capital effects varies a lot by country and by which
  measures are used. The effects are usually real and usually smaller than the theory's
  rhetoric implies.
- It explains reproduction much better than it explains mobility. People do get out,
  and the theory is weakest exactly there.
</Objections>
