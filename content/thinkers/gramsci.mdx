Gramsci wrote the *Prison Notebooks* in Mussolini's prisons, where the prosecutor had
said the aim was to stop his brain working for twenty years. The text is fragmentary
because of the conditions and coded because of the censor, so "the philosophy of praxis"
means Marxism throughout. This is the rare case where you can see the limit of what
could be said on every page of the source.

He was solving a specific problem. Orthodox Marxism predicted that workers in the
advanced West would revolt. They did not. Many of them supported the order that
subordinated them, and fascism won mass consent rather than merely imposing itself.

<Thesis source="Prison Notebooks, written 1929 to 1935">
The ruling class rules by consent, and holds force in reserve.
</Thesis>

## The vocabulary

<Term id="hegemony">Hegemony</Term> is leadership by consent, as distinct from
domination by force. A group is hegemonic when its view of the world has become the
water everyone swims in.

Gramsci splits the state in two. Political society is the coercive apparatus:
government, courts, police, army. <Term id="civil-society">Civil society</Term> is
everything else that shapes what people think is obvious. Hegemony is manufactured in
the second and defended, if it comes to that, by the first.

<Term id="common-sense">Common sense</Term> is his term for the incoherent inherited
worldview ordinary people carry: folklore, religion, fragments of old philosophy, and
ruling-class ideas absorbed without examination. He is not sneering. He also identifies
what he calls good sense, the kernel of practical critical insight inside it, and the
political task is to work on one until it becomes the other.

Traditional intellectuals, the professor and the cleric, imagine themselves floating
above all classes while servicing the existing order.
<Term id="organic-intellectual">Organic intellectuals</Term> come out of a class and
give its raw view of the world coherence and voice. Any group that wants to lead, or to
break someone else's leadership, has to produce its own.

An <Term id="organic-crisis">organic crisis</Term> is what happens when consent breaks
down and force has to come out into the open. Hence the most quoted line in the
notebooks: the old is dying, the new cannot be born, and in the interregnum a great
variety of morbid symptoms appears.

## Psychological underpinning

System justification theory, developed by John Jost, is the closest experimental
analogue: the documented tendency of people, including the people worst served by an
arrangement, to defend and rationalize it. Sometimes the defending gets stronger the
worse the position. Read it next to hegemony and the two illuminate each other.

## Primary works

- *Selections from the Prison Notebooks* (Hoare and Nowell-Smith, 1971). The standard
  English gateway.
- Stuart Hall's essays on Gramsci, for the most useful modern application.
- Perry Anderson, *The Antinomies of Antonio Gramsci*, the sharpest internal critique.

<Objections>
- Hegemony stretches to fit anything. If consent proves hegemony and dissent proves an
  emerging counter-hegemony, no observation could refute it. That is a problem for a
  theory, not a strength.
- The text is fragmentary, which means it can be quoted in support of almost any
  position, and both the left and the right now claim it.
- Perry Anderson shows that Gramsci uses the distinction between the state and civil
  society in at least three incompatible ways, and that the strategic conclusion
  changes depending on which one you take.
- Michels, in the [elite theory](/milieus/elite-theory) cluster, argues that
  organization itself produces a leadership stratum with its own interests. If he is
  right, counter-hegemonic organizing produces a new oligarchy rather than an
  alternative. The tension is unresolved and it is in the
  [open questions](/open-questions).
</Objections>
