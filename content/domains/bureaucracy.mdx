The domain you meet most often and notice least: forms, targets, eligibility criteria,
performance metrics, the drop-down menu with no option that describes your situation.

## Weber's iron cage

Bureaucracy is the most technically efficient form of organization ever devised, and
Weber said so admiringly before describing the cost. Rationalization produces a
disenchanted world run by specialists without spirit, and the cage is built out of
things that each made sense on their own.

## Scott on legibility

**James C. Scott, *Seeing Like a State* (1998)** is the best entry point in this domain.
States cannot govern what they cannot read, so they simplify: cadastral surveys,
permanent surnames, standardized weights, grid cities, scientific forestry. He calls
this <Term id="legibility">legibility</Term>.

The simplification is not neutral, because reality gets reshaped to match the map. The
scientifically managed forest, planted in rows of a single species for countability,
died. Combine legibility with high-modernist ideology, authoritarian power and a
population unable to resist, and you get catastrophe.

<Term id="metis">Mētis</Term> is his word for the practical local knowledge that cannot
be formalized, and it is the thing legibility destroys. It is also the direct ancestor
of [Illich's](/thinkers/illich) conviviality.

In *Domination and the Arts of Resistance* (1990) Scott adds the public transcript and
the <Term id="hidden-transcript">hidden transcript</Term>: what subordinates perform in
front of power, and what they actually say behind it. This is the strongest available
answer to the objection that [Bourdieu](/thinkers/bourdieu) and
[Foucault](/thinkers/foucault) leave no room for agency.

## Graeber on why deregulation produces more paperwork

**David Graeber, *The Utopia of Rules* (2015)** states the iron law of liberalism:
market reforms and deregulation reliably increase total bureaucracy rather than reducing
it, because a market has to be built and policed. He also describes structural
stupidity, produced by asymmetric power: those below have to interpret those above
constantly, and those above never have to reciprocate.

## Goodhart's Law

<Term id="goodharts-law">When a measure becomes a target, it stops being a good
measure.</Term> It is the most portable idea in this section and the operational core of
what goes wrong with metrics in schools, hospitals, policing and platforms. Pair it with
Campbell's Law and with Jerry Muller, *The Tyranny of Metrics* (2018).

Governance by model, which is where a great deal of this begins, has a specific address
and a start date: [RAND](/milieus/rand), Santa Monica, 1948.

## Mills, and the move this whole site is built on

**C. Wright Mills, *The Power Elite* (1956)** describes interlocking military, corporate
and political leadership as a coherent stratum, held together by shared background and
by people circulating between the three, rather than by conspiracy.

*The Sociological Imagination* (1959) is the more important book. The discipline it
teaches, connecting personal troubles to public issues, is the founding move of this
entire project and it is on the [front page](/) for that reason.
