This is the richest seam in the material and the easiest place to start, partly because
the people who built the field wrote it all down and were proud of it. Nothing here had
to be exposed.

## The founding documents

**Walter Lippmann, *Public Opinion* (1922)** coined the phrase
<Term id="manufacture-of-consent">the manufacture of consent</Term>. His argument is
that the public is necessarily governed by stereotypes, the pictures in our heads,
because the real environment is too large and complex to know directly. His conclusion
is elitist rather than critical: since the public cannot govern itself competently, a
specialized class has to manage opinion. Read it as an admission from an insider.

**Edward Bernays, *Propaganda* (1928)**. Freud's nephew and the literal inventor of
public relations. The book opens by stating that the conscious, intelligent manipulation
of the organized habits and opinions of the masses is an essential part of democratic
society, carried out by an invisible governing body. He applied psychoanalytic ideas to
markets: sell the desire, not the product. His Torches of Freedom campaign attached
cigarettes to women's emancipation. He published the method with pride, which makes it
the best primary source in the field.

**Jacques Ellul, *Propaganda* (1962)** is the deepest and the least comfortable.
Propaganda, for Ellul, is not primarily lies. It is a condition of technological mass
society. It works best on the educated, who consume the most information, believe
themselves immune, and need a coherent opinion about everything. He separates agitation
propaganda, which stirs people up, from integration propaganda, the slow kind that
produces conformity to an existing order, and he argues the second matters far more.
Any reading that treats propaganda as something governments do to stupid people should
be corrected by this book.

## The structural model

**Edward Herman and Noam Chomsky, *Manufacturing Consent* (1988)** set out the
<Term id="propaganda-model">propaganda model</Term> and its five filters: ownership,
advertising as the revenue source, dependence on official sources, organized negative
response, and an external enemy or ideological frame.

The key claim is structural rather than conspiratorial. No orders are given. People who
have internalized the constraints are the ones who get promoted, and they are not lying
when they say nobody tells them what to write.

Objections to keep attached: the model is functionalist and hard to falsify, it
underweights how audiences actually read things, and it underweights conflict inside
newsrooms. The media economy it describes, of mass advertising and few outlets, has
changed a great deal since 1988, and what the platform-era version looks like is a live
question rather than a settled one.

## The medium as the message

**Neil Postman, *Amusing Ourselves to Death* (1985)** makes the Huxley-not-Orwell
argument: the danger is not a boot but a medium that makes serious public argument
impossible by converting everything into entertainment. Pair it with Guy Debord, *The
Society of the Spectacle* (1967).

**Adorno and Horkheimer** on the <Term id="culture-industry">culture industry</Term>:
mass culture as standardized industrial production that pacifies, offers
pseudo-individuality, and turns leisure into an extension of work. The story of how that
argument collided with American audience research is in
[the Radio Project](/milieus/radio-project), which is the more useful way in.

<Contested status="Watch the method">
**Adam Curtis** documentaries, *The Century of the Self* above all, are the best known
visual entry point to this material and are worth watching. They are also a case study
in the failure mode: exhilarating, associative, and repeatedly criticized for implying
causation through juxtaposition. Watch them, then check the claims that moved you most.
</Contested>

## Where the money shows

[The Congress for Cultural Freedom](/milieus/congress-for-cultural-freedom) is the
historical case of a well-funded field being shaped without a single lie being told, and
[the Radio Project](/milieus/radio-project) is the case of a funding structure quietly
deciding what a discipline was allowed to find.
