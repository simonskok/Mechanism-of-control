A note on vocabulary before anything else. The phrase "dark psychology" is not used on
this site. It is a self-help label with no scholarly content behind it, and it costs
credibility with exactly the readers worth having. The real material is more disturbing
than the phrase suggests, and all of it is empirically contestable, which is the point.

## Influence, documented by practitioners

**Robert Cialdini, *Influence* (1984)** lists reciprocity, commitment and consistency,
social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, and later unity. It was written as a field
guide for practitioners and is best read defensively, because it documents what the
persuasion industry already does to you.

**Robert Jay Lifton, *Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism* (1961)** gives
eight criteria of thought reform, including milieu control, the demand for purity,
confession, sacred science and
<Term id="loading-the-language">loading the language</Term>. The last one, meaning
thought-terminating clichés that end an argument rather than answering it, is the single
most useful item here for reading media. The criteria apply well beyond cults.

## Obedience, with the caveats that belong to it

<Contested status="Weakened by archival work">
**Stanley Milgram (1961 to 1963)** on obedience to authority. Gina Perry's archival work
in *Behind the Shock Machine* shows the experimenter improvised prods well beyond the
written protocol, many participants doubted the setup was real, and the compliance
figures were selectively presented. The finding survives in weakened form. The popular
version, in which two thirds of people will electrocute a stranger on request, does not.
</Contested>

<Contested status="Present as a cautionary tale, not a finding">
**Zimbardo's Stanford Prison Experiment (1971)** is the most famous psychology study in
the world and it should not be cited as evidence of anything about human nature.
Archival recordings show guards were coached toward brutality, it was never properly
replicated, and it functions today mainly as a demonstration of how a compelling
narrative outruns its data. Including this is a rigor signal, not a concession.
</Contested>

**Solomon Asch (1951)** on conformity to visibly wrong group judgments does replicate
reasonably well, with effect sizes that vary by culture and era.

## Why people defend arrangements that disadvantage them

- <Term id="system-justification">System justification theory</Term> (John Jost). The
  documented tendency to defend the status quo, sometimes more strongly the worse your
  position in it. The closest experimental analogue to Gramsci's hegemony and Bourdieu's
  misrecognition.
- **Just-world hypothesis** (Melvin Lerner). The need to believe outcomes are deserved,
  which produces victim-blaming and self-blame.
- **Cognitive dissonance** (Leon Festinger). Having acted, we revise belief to match the
  act. This is the laboratory version of [Havel's greengrocer](/east-west).
- **Learned helplessness** (Martin Seligman). The empirical form of
  [Illich's](/thinkers/illich) disabling professions.

## Attention and choice architecture

**Kahneman and Tversky** on heuristics, biases and framing effects: identical
information in different frames produces different decisions. Attach the replication
crisis caveat, since priming results in particular have fared badly.

**Thaler and Sunstein, *Nudge* (2008)** should be read against the grain. Presented as
benevolent libertarian paternalism, it is simultaneously a manual for behaviour
modification by states and companies, and it is the most direct contemporary instance of
[Foucault's](/thinkers/foucault)
<Term id="governmentality">governmentality</Term>: not forcing, but arranging the choice
field so that free people choose as designed. The institutional trail is in
[the behavioural state](/milieus/behavioural-state).

**Shoshana Zuboff, *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism* (2019)** on behavioural surplus
and the shift from predicting behaviour to shaping it. The present-day panopticon, with
one important difference: the watching is not felt at all. Criticized for technological
determinism and for treating an advertising business model as a total civilizational
rupture.

## The Frankfurt School layer

**Marcuse, *One-Dimensional Man* (1964)** on repressive tolerance and false needs: an
affluent society that satisfies manufactured desires, absorbs its own opposition, and
produces people unable to imagine an alternative. The paternalism objection is obvious
and worth stating: somebody has to decide which needs are false.
